## How it works

`states` in the definition declares a state by name, `linked`, with two styles:

- its members recolor the contour of nodes and the line of edges with `--graph-accent`, widen both to 2 pixels, show the label and color it;
- `others`, the style of every element outside the state, multiplies the opacity by 0.3 and hides the label, as long as the state has a member.

A state holds no data: the application decides who belongs to it. On `selectionchange`, `graph.paths.reach(key, { maxCost: 1 })` returns the selected node, its neighbors and the edges between them. They go to `linked` with `graph.states.set`, and the rest of the graph fades without a key to list. An empty selection clears the state, and the whole graph comes back.

A free state is drawn in place, so the nodes of the faded graph cover the edges of the neighborhood. `raise: true` on `linked` draws its members again above every mark, as the selection is, and places their labels first. The button rebuilds the definition with `raise` on or off and passes it to `graph.update`. The marks are the same objects, so only the styles of the states change.

The selected node is in both `selected` and `linked`: each property comes from the first state that sets it, so it keeps the halo of the selection.

## Try it

- Click a node in another group: the neighborhood moves and the status counts it.
- Switch to drawn in place, then zoom in: faded nodes now cover the accent edges.
- Click the background to clear the selection and the state.
