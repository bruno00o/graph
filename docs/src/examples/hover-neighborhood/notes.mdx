## How it works

The definition declares three free states: `near` with an accent outline and a label, `link` with an accent stroke, both raised, and `muted` with a low opacity.

On `pointerenter`, a node asks `graph.paths.reach(key, { maxCost: 1 })` for itself, its neighbors and the edges between them. These go into `near` and `link`, everything else into `muted`. An edge keeps its two ends and itself. On `pointerleave`, `graph.states.clear()` brings the whole graph back.

## Try it

- Move the pointer over a node: its neighbors stay, the rest fades.
- Move over an edge: only its two ends stay.
- Leave the element: everything comes back.
