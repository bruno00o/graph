## How it works

Each chain of nodes runs from one base, in green, to another. It is not a cycle on its own: the two bases close it. When a chain ends on two different bases, every node on it has two ways out.

[`ways`](/docs/api/ways), from `@bruno00o/graph/ways`, finds these chains: it adds a virtual node joined once to each exit, so a chain between two exits becomes a cycle through it. One depth-first walk from the virtual node splits the graph into biconnected components (Hopcroft and Tarjan, 1973). A node in the component of the virtual node has two paths that share no node and end on two different exits (Menger's theorem). The rest hangs on a single node: light nodes and dashed edges. `pair` returns those two paths for a node, as a flow of two units from the node to the virtual node, every node split in two so that the ways share none (Suurballe and Tarjan, 1984).

The [`chains`](/docs/api/chains) layout, from `@bruno00o/graph/layouts/chains`, places the graph from that analysis, not from a field of the data:

- A chain between two exits becomes an arc of a circle through both, `nodes + 1` steps long, a step being the node diameter plus `spacing`; the dotted line shows the pair of exits that closes it. A chain that comes back to its own exit becomes a circle under it.
- Exits that share a chain or a direct link form a unit: two exits sit 1.6 steps apart (A and B, C and D), more in a file on a line spaced so that each chain makes a half circle (E, F and G), and only a cycle or a fork in the exits, an exit linked to three others or more, makes a heart placed on a circle or by `core`.
- Branches fan out from the node they hang on, each sibling getting an angle in proportion to its subtree (Eades, 1992; Lin and Yen, 2005); a branch with a single child per level moves one step per node, like a chain. The units sit in rows.

Two traps in the data:

- The second chain between C and D lacks one link: each half hangs on one base and is drawn as a branch above it.
- The chain on H comes back to the base it left: it looks like a ring, but everything on it goes through H.

On `click`, the two ways of a node come from `pair`, a node with one way out shows it with `graph.paths.shortest` up to the node it goes through, then the two ways of that node. The states `first`, `second` and `single` color the ways, and `route` fades everything else.

## Try it

- Click a node of the ring between A and B: one way to each base.
- Click node 7: one way out up to node 2, then the two ways of node 2.
- Click a node above C or D, then one on the ring of H.
- Click the background to clear.
