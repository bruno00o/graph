## How it works

The definition draws three things besides the marks:

- `background: { grid: 'lines' }` draws a grid under everything. Its spacing changes by powers of 4 with the zoom, so lines never crowd, and every fourth line is darker.
- `hulls` holds one `hull()`: one hull per group of the node mark, or one around the `selected` state. Each hull follows its nodes while the force layout runs and while you drag.
- The force layout places the nodes, since the data gives them no coordinates.

Three kinds of HTML follow the graph through `graph.anchors.set`, without any code per frame:

- The toolbar follows the `selected` state, above the bounding box of the selected nodes, and stays hidden while nothing is selected. It is `interactive`, so its buttons take clicks, while the wheel still zooms the graph.
- The tooltip follows the node or edge under the pointer, with `when: 'hovered'` so it hides as soon as the pointer leaves.
- Each group name sits above the bounding box of the keys of its group.

## Try it

- Press **Background** to switch between lines, dots and no grid, then zoom: the grid refines and coarsens.
- Press **Hulls** for hulls around the selection, then click nodes with Shift held.
- Press **Shape** to bend the sides of every hull into arcs.
- Drag a node out of its group: its hull stretches to keep it.
