---
title: orthogonalRouting
description: Edge routing in horizontal and vertical segments around nodes, computed in a Web Worker.
---

```ts
import { orthogonalRouting } from '@bruno00o/graph/routing'

function orthogonalRouting(options?: OrthogonalRoutingOptions): EdgeRouting
```

Returns a frozen [`EdgeRouting`](/docs/api/define-graph#routing) named `'orthogonal'`. Pass it as `routing` to `defineGraph`. See [Edge routing](/docs/routing) for a walkthrough.

## Options

```ts
type RouteAxis = 'auto' | 'vertical' | 'horizontal'
```

<api-table path="routing/index.ts" name="OrthogonalRoutingOptions" />

`gap`, `spacing` and `radius` take values of 0 or more; a negative value is raised to 0 and a non-finite value falls back to its default. `maxEdges` is rounded down, `Infinity` sets no limit, a negative value is raised to 0, and `NaN` falls back to its default. Any other `axis` throws a `TypeError`.

## Result

<api-table path="routing/index.ts" name="OrthogonalRoutingSettings" />

`routing.options` holds the resolved settings, and `routing.radius` and `routing.maxEdges` the other two options. Two `orthogonalRouting()` calls with the same settings and `maxEdges` are equal for the graph: a new definition with an equal routing does not compute again, and one that only changes `radius` redraws the corners.

## Behavior

- Each node and group container is an obstacle, grown by `gap`. An edge may cross the containers that hold its source or its target, and goes around every other box.
- A route leaves its source and enters its target in a straight line from their centers, and bends only outside the two nodes.
- An edge first tries the routes with one or two bends between the chosen sides of its ends. When they are blocked, or when their ends are too close across the `axis` to leave through those sides, it searches a grid drawn from the sides of the obstacles around its ends for the route of least cost: its length, plus a fixed cost for each bend and a larger one for entering or leaving a node through a side off the `axis`. A route takes a side off the `axis` only when the sides on the `axis` are blocked or lead to a much longer route. The search covers a window around both ends, widened once when it finds nothing.
- An edge with no free route keeps its first simple route, across the boxes in its way. This route can leave or enter a node through a side off the `axis`.
- Segments that share a line are then moved apart by `spacing`, in an order that keeps routes from crossing where it can, within the free space of their channel. Ends spread along the middle of their side.
- The result is the same for the same positions, sizes and edges.
- The computation runs in a Web Worker shipped inside the package, created from a `blob:` URL. Without `Worker`, when the content security policy refuses `worker-src blob:`, or when the worker fails, it runs on the main thread in slices of 8 milliseconds.
