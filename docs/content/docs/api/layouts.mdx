```ts
import { geo, grid, layers, placed, radial, rings } from '@bruno00o/graph/layouts'

function layers<T = unknown>(options?: LayersOptions<T>): Layout<T>
function grid<T = unknown>(options?: GridOptions<T>): Layout<T>
function rings<T = unknown>(options?: RingsOptions<T>): Layout<T>
function radial(options?: RadialOptions): Layout
function geo<T = unknown>(options?: GeoOptions<T>): Layout<T>
function placed<T = unknown>(positions: PlacedPositions<T>): Layout<T>
```

Each returns a frozen [`Layout`](/docs/api/define-graph#layout) named `'layers'`, `'grid'`, `'rings'`, `'radial'`, `'geo'` or `'placed'`, with `incremental: false`. Pass it as `layout` to `defineGraph`, or to `graph.layout.run` for a single run. See [Layouts](/docs/layout#layers) for a walkthrough.

## Shared behavior

- Only free nodes move: alive, without `x` and `y` in the data, chosen by the run, and not being dragged.
- Moved nodes are centered on their current center, so a run on part of the graph stays where that part was. `geo` and `placed` are the exceptions: they place nodes by their coordinates.
- With `others: 'ignored'` in the [run options](/docs/api/graph-instance#layout), the nodes left out of `keys` are not in the graph a layout reads: `layers` and `radial` take no edge to them, `geo` sees no obstacle there, and containers keep only the nodes laid out. `grid` and `rings` place the same nodes either way.
- Every spacing option is a gap between node borders, in world units, as in `force`. `grid`, `rings` and `radial` give every node a place of the same size, so their `spacing` is the gap between two of the largest moved nodes: neighboring places are that diameter plus `spacing` apart. It defaults to the largest diameter, and at least 24. Default gaps come from the nodes only, never from [blocks](#blocks-and-containers).
- A negative or non-finite spacing falls back to its default. `0` is allowed: the largest nodes touch.
- The result ends without progress, in a microtask: nodes glide to it over `transitions.layout`, or the `duration` of the run.
- When nodes arrive, every free node is laid out again.
- `geo` places every node by its coordinates, then pushes apart the containers of unfolded groups that overlap, 16 units from their neighbors.
- `grid`, `rings` and `radial` lay unfolded groups out as blocks, from the deepest up: each group with the same layout, then its blocks in the group above, each standing in as one node the size of its container. Containers that still overlap are then pushed apart, 16 units from their neighbors. `layers` places groups in the same layers as the rest of the graph, see below. See [groups in layouts](/docs/layout#groups).

## layers

```ts
type LayersDirection = 'down' | 'up' | 'right' | 'left'
```

<api-table path="layouts/index.ts" name="LayersOptions" />

- **Cycles.** Edges that close a cycle are turned around, as few as a greedy pass can find. They are still drawn, pointing back. Duplicate edges count once.
- **Layers.** Each node gets a layer so that every edge goes to a later one, with the smallest total length (network simplex). Each component starts at the first layer.
- **Order.** Nodes of a layer are sorted by the mean position of their neighbors, sweeping down and up while crossings drop. An edge that skips layers holds a place in each layer it crosses.
- **Placement.** Without groups, each node settles where the sum of its squared distances to its neighbors on the layers next to it is smallest, its radius and `nodeSpacing` away from the nodes beside it. Edges that skip layers pull harder, so they run almost straight, and the drawing stays about as wide as its widest layer. With unfolded groups, each node is instead aligned with its neighbors where it can be, and pushed aside where it cannot, so that every container stays a single box: the four alignments, from each side and each direction, are balanced.
- **Components.** Separate components and lone nodes are packed in rows, the largest first, `layerSpacing` apart. The length of the rows is the one that fits the whole drawing in the smallest square.
- **Groups.** Unfolded groups get layers with the rest of the graph: each group spans the layers from its first member to its last, and the layering keeps that span short, the more so for groups with many members. On every layer, the members of a group are side by side, nested groups inside, and sibling groups keep the same order from one layer to the next. Each container leaves its `padding` around its members and the room of its label on the side where it is drawn, both on top of `nodeSpacing` and `layerSpacing`. An edge that skips layers stays inside the group of its source while that group lasts, then joins the group of its target, so it crosses borders on the ends of the containers. When some nodes do not move, or a container is a circle, containers are then pushed apart, 16 units from their neighbors.
- Edges to nodes that do not move, because they have `x` and `y` in the data or were left out of the run, are ignored.
- The same graph always gives the same result.

### Ranks

With `rank`, the layers come from the data instead of the edges, and the rest of the steps stay the same.

- **Layers.** Only the order of the values counts: the smallest value goes on the first layer, the next distinct value on the second, and so on, so gaps, negative and fractional values leave no empty layer. Nodes whose value is missing, `null` or not a finite number go together on one more layer after the last.
- **Edges.** An edge from a higher rank to a lower one is laid out turned around, and still drawn toward its target. An edge between two nodes of the same layer is left out: its ends are placed without it, and it is drawn straight along the layer. Edges that skip layers hold a place in each layer they cross, as without ranks.
- **One set of layers.** The whole graph shares the layers: separate components sit side by side on the same layers instead of being packed in rows, so a layer is one line across the drawing.
- **`root`** is ignored, and no cycle needs breaking.
- **Groups.** Each unfolded group spans the layers of its members, as without ranks.
- **Time.** The layering step is skipped. With ranks that follow the edges, 10,000 nodes and 15,000 edges take about 40 milliseconds instead of 65, and 50,000 nodes about 200 instead of 300. Ranks that ignore the edges make edges skip layers, each holding a place in every layer it crosses: with 8 random ranks, the same graphs take about 110 and 360 milliseconds.

## Order

```ts
type NodeOrder<T> = 'degree' | ((a: T, b: T) => number)
```

`'degree'` puts the nodes with the most edges first. A function compares the items of two nodes, as given to `nodes()`; ties keep the internal order. `T` is the item type of the node marks. `graph.layout.run` infers it from the definition; inside `defineGraph`, pass it as the type argument of `grid`, `rings` or `geo`, or annotate a parameter. The same goes for the `rank` of `layers` and the function given to `placed`.

## radial

<api-table path="layouts/index.ts" name="RadialOptions" />

- A breadth-first walk from the roots, through free nodes only, gives each node its ring and the node it was reached from. Edges count in both directions.
- Each branch gets a slice of the circle in proportion to its number of leaves, and each node sits in the middle of its slice. When a ring is crowded, it grows and its nodes spread apart in the same order.
- A single fixed root keeps its place and the rings form around it. A single free root goes to the center of the moved nodes.
- Nodes the walk does not reach go on an outer ring, evenly spaced.

## geo

<api-table path="layouts/index.ts" name="GeoOptions" />

- **Projection.** A node at `longitude` and `latitude` goes to `(geoToWorld(point) − geoToWorld(center)) × scale`: the Web Mercator world of a map, 512 units wide, moved so that `center` is at the origin and multiplied by `scale`. Latitudes are clamped to ±85.05, as on a map. A coordinate that is not a finite number counts as missing.
- **Scale.** Without `scale`, the layout looks for the smallest scale at which about one distinct place in five would share a spot, the size of a typical node plus `spacing`, with another place. The search stays within a factor 4 below and 32 above the scale that would spread the places evenly over their box, so a few tight clusters far apart give dense clusters, not a huge empty graph.
- **Center.** Without `center`, the middle of the box around the places is drawn at the origin. Coordinates stay small, which keeps their precision at any scale.
- **Spread.** Places are taken from the most crowded area first, then the places that hold the most nodes. A node whose spot is free stays there. Otherwise it takes the nearest free spot close by, else a free spot at the edge of the crowd it ran into, on the side facing its place. No two nodes end closer than their radii plus `spacing`, fixed nodes included; only containers of groups can be pushed later.
- **Same place.** Two to six nodes at the same coordinates sit on a ring around it, the first on top, then clockwise. More sit on a spiral from the place outward, in the same order.
- **No coordinates.** A node without coordinates goes at the mean of its placed neighbors, or the free spot nearest to it, and the walk goes on through its own neighbors. Nodes the walk cannot reach line up in a grid, right of the others and level with their top.
- Fixed nodes, with `x` and `y` in the data or left out of the run, keep their place and count as placed neighbors and as obstacles.
- The same data and options always give the same result.

## placed

```ts
type PlacedPositions<T = unknown> =
  | Iterable<readonly [Key, Point]>
  | ((datum: T) => Point | null | undefined)
```

`placed` runs positions your code computed as a layout: a run with it resolves when nodes arrive, emits `layoutstart`, `layoutend` and `positionchange` with the origin `'layout'`, glides over `transitions.layout` or the `duration` of the run, frames with `fit`, and stops with `layout.stop`.

- Positions are in world units. Entries are read once, when `placed` is called: a `Map`, the result of [`positions.snapshot`](/docs/api/graph-instance#positions) or of `layout.compute` work as they are. A function reads the item of each free node, as given to `nodes()`.
- A free node without a position, or with an `x` or `y` that is not finite, stays where it is. Nodes that are not free, with `x` and `y` in the data, dragged, or left out of `keys`, never move.
- Its `options` hold the positions you passed: in a definition, the same `Map` or function does not lay out again, a new one does.

## Blocks and containers

When the graph a layout reads has [`blocks`](/docs/api/define-graph#layout), nodes that each stand for a group laid out on its own, the built-in layouts treat them as follows. Without blocks, nothing changes.

| Layout | Blocks | `turns` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `force` | A block pushes the other nodes as hard as all its nodes would: its weight is its `size`. Coarse levels add the weights up. | `'free'` |
| `grid`, `rings`, `radial` | Places are as large as the largest block, but the default `spacing` comes from the plain nodes, at least 24. | `'none'` for `grid`, `'free'` for the others |
| `layers` | Blocks take their room in their layer; the default `nodeSpacing` and `layerSpacing` come from the plain nodes, at least 8 and 24. | `'flip'` |
| `geo` | A block without a place of its own goes to the mean of the places of its nodes, in Web Mercator. The typical size that sets the default `spacing` and `scale` comes from the plain nodes. | `'none'` |
| `placed` | A block without a position of its own goes to the mean of the positions given to its nodes, read by key or through the function; with none, it stays where it is. | `'none'` |

The groups of [`container.layout`](/docs/layout#a-layout-per-group) reach the layouts this way: each group with a layout of its own is a block in the layout around it, and its members are laid out with `container` set. After the layout around them, a block is turned to face its neighbors as its layout's `turns` allows.

Inside a [container](/docs/api/define-graph#layout), `force` pulls the nodes toward their center with `cohesion` instead of `gravity`, and takes 120 steps below 1,000 nodes unless `steps` is given: a group laid out on its own stays as compact as it would among the rest of the graph. The other layouts do not read `container`.
