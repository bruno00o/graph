```ts
import { exportMapPng, mapSurface, mountMapGraph } from '@bruno00o/graph/map'

function mountMapGraph<S extends string = string, M extends Mark = Mark>(
  map: Map,
  options: MapGraphOptions<S, M>,
): GraphInstance<S, M>

function mapSurface(map: Map, options?: MapSurfaceOptions): GraphSurface

function exportMapPng<S extends string>(
  graph: GraphInstance<S>,
  options?: MapPngOptions,
): Promise<Blob>
```

`map` is a MapLibre GL JS `Map`, version 6 or later. `mountMapGraph` adds a custom layer to its style and returns a [`GraphInstance`](/docs/api/graph-instance). See [Map](/docs/map) for a walkthrough.

## mountMapGraph options

<api-table path="map/index.ts" name="MapGraphOptions" />

The other options are those of [`mountGraph`](/docs/api/mount-graph#options), except `initialView`, `view` and `onViewChange`: the map holds the view. A [`signal`](/docs/api/mount-graph#signal) that aborts destroys the graph and removes its layer; a signal already aborted leaves the map untouched. In React, use [`<MapGraph>`](/docs/api/map-graph).

## mapSurface

`mapSurface` makes a surface on the map that a graph mounted by `mountGraph` can move to: pass it as `surface` to [`mountGraph`](/docs/api/mount-graph#options) or to [`graph.update`](/docs/api/graph-instance), and `null` to bring the graph back to its own canvas. See [With and without a map](/docs/map#with-and-without-a-map).

<api-table path="map/index.ts" name="MapSurfaceOptions" />

<api-table path="index.ts" name="GraphSurface" />

- A surface holds one graph at a time: moving a second graph onto it throws an `Error`, and so does a surface whose map was removed.
- The layer and its renderer are added at the first visit and kept after the graph leaves. `release()` removes them, and brings back to its canvas a graph that is on the surface. `graph.destroy()` releases every surface the graph visited.
- `map.remove()` while a graph is on the surface brings the graph back to its canvas, and logs a warning in development.
- While a graph is on the surface, everything of the [behavior](#behavior) below applies to it, as to a graph mounted by `mountMapGraph`. On its canvas again, its layout, background, bundling, routing and lens come back.
- `mountGraph(container, { surface })` mounts the graph straight on the map; its canvas is made the first time it moves back. `mountMapGraph(map, options)` is `mountGraph(map.getContainer(), { ...options, surface: mapSurface(map, { id, beforeId }) })`, with the surface released with the graph.

## Errors

- `exportMapPng` throws an `Error` for a destroyed graph or a graph that is not on a map, and a `RangeError` for a `pixelRatio` number that is not positive or a map smaller than one pixel.
- `exportMapPng` rejects when the hidden map fails to load its style. Tiles that are still missing after `timeout` are left out of the image.

## Behavior

- The graph follows the map's camera at every frame of the map, and draws nothing when the map does not render: an idle map costs nothing. `graph.drawn()` resolves once the map has drawn the layer.
- `graph.view.set`, `fit`, `zoomBy` and the keyboard move the map with `jumpTo`, one step per frame for animated calls. `update({ view })` is ignored while the graph stays on the map, unless it comes with a new `surface`: that `view` is then the one to take on the other side, and on a map it moves the map once the graph has arrived.
- During a node drag or a lasso, the map's drag pan and box zoom are off. Over a node, its double click zoom is off. They come back as they were.
- `destroy` removes the layer and the graph element, and gives back rotation, pitch and the largest pitch of the map. When the style changes, the layer is added again.
- The graph follows the flat and the globe projections, and the change from one to the other. On the globe, elements on the far side are hidden and never picked.
- A layout, `background` and `bundling` in the definition are ignored, and `graph.layout.run` rejects with an `Error`. In development, a layout logs a warning once. The lens and the minimap are not supported while the graph is on a map: a minimap of a map graph draws nothing, starts no worker and warns once in development. A graph moved back to its canvas with `update({ surface })` draws its minimap and lens again, and runs its layout again. Hulls, groups and the legend work; the `padding` of group containers is in pixels.
