A shape editor lets the pointer and the keys draw new [shapes](/docs/shapes), move them, drag their points, add and remove points, resize them by the handles of their box, and undo. The editor never changes your data: it proposes each change as an edit, and your code writes it to the data, or not. Until the data answers, the editor draws the shape as proposed.

```ts
import { mountGraph } from '@bruno00o/graph'
import { applyShapeEdits, createShapeEditor, mountShapeEditor } from '@bruno00o/graph/editor'

let zones = initialZones
const graph = mountGraph(element, { definition: define(zones), ariaLabel: 'Zones' })

const editor = createShapeEditor(graph, {
  onEdit: (edits) => {
    zones = applyShapeEdits(zones, edits)
    graph.update({ definition: define(zones) })
  },
})
mountShapeEditor(editor)
```

`createShapeEditor` takes the pointer and the keys of the graph; `mountShapeEditor` draws the shapes being edited and their handles over the graph, in an SVG layer, and the input of the [text](#text) being typed. Both come from `@bruno00o/graph/editor`, so a graph without an editor carries none of its code. Every option is in the [API reference](/docs/api/editor).

## Moving and reshaping

| Gesture | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Press a shape and drag | Selects it and moves it, with the other selected shapes |
| Shift, Cmd or Ctrl and press | Adds the shape to the selection; a click on a selected shape removes it |
| Click a selected shape | Keeps only that shape selected |
| Drag a point of the selected shape | Moves the point |
| Press a midpoint and drag | Adds a point there, and moves it |
| Alt and click a point | Removes the point, down to 3 points for an area and 2 for a line |
| Drag a handle of the box | Resizes the selected shapes from the opposite handle; Shift keeps the proportions |
| Arrow keys | Move the selected shapes by 1 pixel, 10 with Shift |
| Delete or Backspace | Removes the point last pressed, or else the selected shapes |
| Escape | Cancels the gesture; without one, clears the selection as usual |
| Hold Space and drag | Moves the view, even from a shape |

Points show on a single selected area or line. A rectangle, a circle and a selection of several shapes show the 8 handles of their box instead. A circle keeps its proportions, so it stays round. When the points of an outline are closer than 6 pixels on average on the screen, or more than 1,000 of them show, only the point and the midpoint nearest the pointer get a handle: zoom in to reach the others.

A press away from the shapes the editor can edit goes to the graph as before: nodes drag, the background moves the view, and Shift and drag draws the [lasso](/docs/interaction#lasso). A still press on a shape emits `click`, as without the editor; a drag emits neither `click` nor `dragstart`. With touch, a second finger cancels the gesture and pinches.

The selection is the selection of the graph: `graph.selection.get().shapes`, `selectionchange` and a [controlled selection](/docs/interaction#controlled-selection) work as before, and `editor.store.state.selected` lists the selected shapes that the editor can edit. A shape that is not shown, in a [hidden layer](/docs/multilayer), out of its [zoom range](/docs/zoom) or of the [time window](/docs/time), or hidden by a state, stays selected but leaves `selected`: it has no handles and no proposed outline, the keys and the methods called without keys leave it alone, and a gesture whose shape hides is cancelled, as with Escape. With `interaction: { select: false }`, a press moves a shape without selecting it; with `'single'`, Shift does not add.

## Drawing shapes

`editor.setTool(tool)` chooses what a press does: `'select'`, the default, selects, moves and reshapes; `'rectangle'`, `'circle'`, `'line'`, `'polygon'` and `'freehand'` draw, and `'text'` draws a box to [type in](#text). `editor.store.state.tool` holds the current tool, for a toolbar to show it.

| Tool | Gesture |
| --- | --- |
| Rectangle | Drag from a corner to the opposite one, or click both corners; Shift makes a square |
| Circle | Drag from the center to the edge, or click the center then the edge |
| Line | Click point by point, then Enter or a double click; a drag draws a single segment |
| Polygon | Click point by point, then Enter, a double click or a click on the first point |
| Freehand | Drag: the stroke closes into an area when its ends meet, and stays a line otherwise |
| Text | Drag or click two corners, like a rectangle, then type |

Two clicks work everywhere a drag does, so that a shape never needs a drag. With a line or a polygon, Shift turns the next segment by steps of 15 degrees, Backspace, Delete or Cmd or Ctrl and Z take back the last point, and Escape drops the shape. A freehand stroke keeps a point every 2 pixels while drawing, then drops the points closer than half a pixel to its outline.

While a drawing tool is active it takes every press, on shapes and nodes too, except with Space held, which still moves the view, and with another button than the main one. The double click that ends a line or a polygon reaches neither the `doubleclick` event nor a group to fold. Choose `'select'` to give the pointer back to the graph.

A finished shape is an `'add'` edit, with the key from `newKey`, `crypto.randomUUID()` by default, and the mark from `newMark`, the first name of `marks` by default, else the first shape mark of the graph. The editor selects it and draws it until your data has it, as for any other edit.

```ts
const editor = createShapeEditor(graph, {
  tool: 'polygon',
  newKey: () => `zone-${++count}`,
  onEdit: (edits) => {
    zones = applyShapeEdits(zones, edits)
    graph.update({ definition: define(zones) })
  },
})
```

Drawn shapes are in world units, rectangles as `{ x, y, width, height }`, circles as `{ center, radius }`, lines and areas as `points` and `rings`. On a map, `units: 'auto'` draws in degrees instead: GeoJSON `Polygon` and `LineString`, counterclockwise rings closed by their first point, and circles around a place with a radius in meters. `units: 'world'` and `'degrees'` choose one or the other everywhere. `'auto'` follows a graph that [moves onto a map and back](/docs/map#with-and-without-a-map), shape by shape.

### Snapping

`snap: { grid }` snaps the points to a grid: a spacing in world units, or `'background'` for the lines of the background grid shown at the current zoom, which needs a `background` grid in the definition: without one, nothing snaps, and a development build warns about it once. A map has no background grid, so give `snap.grid` a spacing there. Drawn points snap, and so do dragged points, resized boxes and moved shapes, by the top left corner of their box. Shift turns segments by steps of `snap.angle`, 15 degrees by default.

```ts
editor.update({ snap: { grid: 'background' } })
```

### Copies and order

`editor.duplicate()`, or Cmd or Ctrl and D, proposes copies of the selected shapes 16 pixels lower and to the right, as `'add'` edits that carry the original item in `datum`, and selects them. `editor.raise()` and `editor.lower()` draw the selected shapes above or under the other shapes of their mark: they propose `'change'` edits with a new `order`, which `applyShapeEdits` writes to the `order` field. Read it through the `order` option of the mark:

```ts
shapes(zones, { name: 'zones', order: 'order' })
```

A shape already above every other shape of its mark makes `raise` propose nothing, and the same for `lower` at the bottom. `editor.store.state.canRaise` and `canLower` say whether the call would propose something for the selection, as `canUndo` does for `undo`, so that a toolbar can grey its buttons:

```ts
editor.store.subscribe(() => {
  const { canRaise, canLower } = editor.store.state
  raiseButton.disabled = !canRaise
  lowerButton.disabled = !canLower
})
```

## Text

A shape has text when its mark has a [`label`](/docs/shapes#labels). Double click it, or press Enter while it is the only selected shape, and an input opens over it, with the text of its label, in its font and at its size on the screen. The label hides while you type. Enter starts a new line; Escape, Cmd or Ctrl and Enter, a click elsewhere or another tool closes the input and proposes a `'change'` edit with the new `text`, which `applyShapeEdits` writes to the `text` field. `editor.cancel()` closes it with no edit.

The `'text'` tool draws a box, then opens the input in it. Closing it proposes an `'add'` edit with `tool: 'text'` and the text, for the mark of `textMark`, `newMark` by default; a box left empty proposes nothing. Give the text mark a label that reads the `text` field, wraps and scales with the box:

```ts
const editor = createShapeEditor(graph, {
  textMark: 'notes',
  onEdit: (edits) => {
    zones = applyShapeEdits(zones, edits, { mark: 'zones' })
    notes = applyShapeEdits(notes, edits, { mark: 'notes' })
    graph.update({ definition: define(zones, notes) })
  },
})
mountShapeEditor(editor, { placeholder: messages.typeHere })

shapes(notes, {
  name: 'notes',
  fill: 'transparent',
  label: { text: 'text', wrap: true, scale: 'world', place: 'always' },
})
```

When the text needs more lines than the box holds, the same edit makes the box taller, its top edge in place, so that the label shows whole; the input grows while you type. The library writes no text of its own in the input: `placeholder` is yours. `editor.editText(key)`, `setText` and `endText` drive the same input from your code, and `editor.store.state.text` gives its text, its box on the screen and its font, to draw your own input instead of the one of `mountShapeEditor`. An undo puts the previous text back.

## Undo and redo

The editor keeps its own edits: `editor.undo()` and `editor.redo()`, or Cmd or Ctrl and Z, Shift and Cmd or Ctrl and Z, and Ctrl and Y, propose the edits that take them back or make them again. Undoing a removal proposes an `'add'` with the same key and the removed item in `datum`; undoing a drawn shape proposes its removal. `editor.store.state.canUndo` and `canRedo` say whether there is something to undo or redo, for buttons to follow.

```ts
editor.store.subscribe(() => {
  undoButton.disabled = !editor.store.state.canUndo
})
```

The undo is selective: an edit whose shapes changed since, by another user, by your code or because the data declined it, is skipped and forgotten, so that undo never brings back a state over a change it did not make. The editor remembers 100 edits by default, none with `history: 0`. Changes that do not come from the editor are not in its history: keep your own for them.

## Writing edits back

Each gesture ends with one batch of edits, given to `onEdit` and to the `edit` event. An edit is an `'add'` with the new `geometry`, a `'change'` with the new `geometry` or `order`, or a `'remove'`, with the `key` and the `name` of the mark; a change and a removal carry the item before the change, `datum`:

```ts
editor.on('edit', ({ edits }) => {
  for (const edit of edits) {
    if (edit.type === 'add') create(edit.key, edit.geometry, edit.datum)
    if (edit.type === 'change') save(edit.key, edit.geometry, edit.order)
    if (edit.type === 'remove') erase(edit.key)
  }
})
```

The new geometry keeps the form of the old one: `{ x, y }` points stay objects with their other fields, `[x, y]` pairs keep their extra values, GeoJSON keeps its type, its other fields and the closing point of each ring, and degrees stay degrees, with longitudes from -180 to 180. The points that did not move are the same values as before. GeoJSON `bbox` is dropped, since it would be stale.

With several shape marks, narrow an edit on its `mark` and its `datum` takes the item type of that mark:

```ts
const editor = createShapeEditor(graph, {
  onEdit: (edits) => {
    for (const edit of edits) {
      if (edit.type === 'add' && edit.mark === 'zones') copyLabel(edit.datum?.label)
      if (edit.type === 'remove' && edit.mark === 'notes') forget(edit.datum.text)
    }
  },
})
```

`applyShapeEdits(items, edits, fields)` applies a batch to an array and returns a new one: the changed items are copies, the others the same objects, so that the graph updates only what changed, and the new items come last, copies of `datum` when the edit has one. It reads keys from `id`, writes outlines to `geometry` and orders to `order` by default; with several shape marks, pass `mark` to take the edits of one. A mark whose `geometry` is a function needs its own writer.

### Pending edits

Until the data of a shape changes, the editor draws it as proposed and hides the shape of the graph, so that a slow save does not make it jump back. `editor.store.state.pending` lists these shapes. When the data does not change within `hold`, 10 seconds by default, the editor shows the data again and emits `editlost` with the edits: the save failed, or your code declined the edit. `hold: Infinity` waits for good, and so does a `hold` longer than a timer can wait, about 24.8 days.

```ts
const editor = createShapeEditor(graph, { hold: 5000 })
editor.on('editlost', ({ edits }) => notify(edits.length))
```

## Saving edits

With a [TanStack DB collection](/docs/tanstack-db) as the source of the shapes, `persistShapes` from `@bruno00o/graph/db` writes each edit into it, and returns a function that stops writing:

```ts tab="Vanilla" tab-group="framework"
import { persistShapes } from '@bruno00o/graph/db'
import { createShapeEditor, mountShapeEditor } from '@bruno00o/graph/editor'

const editor = createShapeEditor(graph)
mountShapeEditor(editor)
const stop = persistShapes(editor, { collection: zones })
```

```tsx tab="React" tab-group="framework"
import { persistShapes } from '@bruno00o/graph/db'
import { useShapeEditor } from '@bruno00o/graph/editor/react'
import { useEffect } from 'react'

function SavedShapes() {
  const { editor } = useShapeEditor()
  useEffect(() => (editor ? persistShapes(editor, { collection: zones }) : undefined), [editor])
  return null
}
```

- A change writes only the fields it changes, `geometry` by default, so `transaction.mutations[i].changes` holds the outline alone. A server that merges rows field by field keeps a color that someone else changed meanwhile. `fields` names the fields of your rows.
- With several shape marks, name one with `mark`: only its edits are written, and `create` receives the new shapes of that mark, with the `datum` type of its items.
- A removal deletes the row. `raise` and `lower` write the `order` field. A new shape, drawn or copied, needs `create`, which builds its whole row from the edit, the copied item in `edit.datum` for a copy. Without `create`, the undo of a removal still inserts the removed row again, its `datum` with the outline of the edit; other new shapes are left out.
- The write is optimistic: the collection holds the new outline at once, and its change ends the wait of the editor in the next frame. Any change of the row ends the wait, so a server that rounds the points or turns the rings does not leave the shape waiting.
- Without `mutationFn`, the collection persists the writes through its own handlers. With `mutationFn`, `createPacedMutations` calls it at the pace of `strategy`, and a mutation that rejects rolls the rows back and emits `editlost` with the edits it carried.
- `pendingState` names a free state that holds each shape from its write until the write is done or has failed, to show the saves in flight.
- To write edits yourself with `collection.update`, assign `edit.geometry` to the draft as it is: the geometry the editor hands out is a fresh, writable copy (`WritableShapeGeometry`), so it needs no cast. Let `onEdit` infer the type of its edits, or annotate `ShapeEdit<Mark, WritableShapeGeometry>`; the plain `ShapeEdit` keeps a read-only geometry.

### While a shape moves

By default, a gesture writes once, on release: the other clients see the shape jump there. With `live: true`, the shapes of the gesture are written every 100 milliseconds while it runs, or every `live` milliseconds, and the release is written at once:

```ts
persistShapes(editor, { collection: zones, live: true })
```

Each write during the gesture costs the write itself, and the reading of the new outlines by every client, yours included, while the editor keeps drawing your shapes under the pointer. When a write takes long, as for a thousand shapes moved together, the next one waits four times as long, so that the writes take at most a fifth of the time. A cancelled gesture writes the shapes back as they were: Escape, a second finger, `editor.cancel()`, or the removal of one of its shapes.

### Two people on one shape

The last writer wins, shape by shape, with no merge of points: two outlines merged point by point can cross. A change of a shape from elsewhere does not stop your gesture, and your release writes over it. A removal from elsewhere ends your gesture, and nothing is written for that shape.

## Who else is editing

`showPresence` from `@bruno00o/graph/db` shows the other people editing the same graph: a cursor at the pointer of each, and their selections in colors of their own. It works with any graph, not only with a shape editor. The library sends nothing: you give it a collection with one row for each person, and the collection carries the rows, through its handlers, a sync engine or, as in the example, local storage.

```ts
import { showPresence } from '@bruno00o/graph/db'

const crowd = createCollection(
  localStorageCollectionOptions({
    id: 'crowd',
    storageKey: 'crowd',
    getKey: (seen: Seen) => seen.id,
  }),
)

const presence = showPresence(graph, {
  collection: crowd,
  me: sessionId,
  create: (id, values) => ({ id, name: userName, ...values }),
})
```

A row holds the `pointer` in world units, the `selection` as keys of `nodes`, `edges` and `shapes`, the `time` of its last write and the `surface` the graph draws on, `'canvas'` or `'map'`; `fields` names yours. `create` builds the row of the local person the first time, with the four values, and the fields of your own, such as a name. `me` is its key. The pointer is written in world units, so a cursor lands on the same place for everyone as long as they see the same world: positions in the data, a map, or a layout with a result that does not depend on the client.

The selections show in free states that you declare, `presence1` to `presence8` by default, each with the color you give it. A person takes the first free one and keeps it while present, and beyond eight people share them. The states of the graph that you did not declare are left out, so a graph with three states shows three colors.

```ts
const look = (color: string) => ({
  nodes: { outline: color, outlineWidth: 3 },
  shapes: { stroke: color, strokeWidth: 4 },
})

defineGraph({
  marks,
  states: {
    presence1: look('var(--graph-cat-1)'),
    presence2: look('var(--graph-cat-2)'),
    presence3: look('var(--graph-cat-3)'),
  },
})
```

The cursors are elements in the [anchor layer](/docs/drawing-layers#anchored-html): a colored dot without text by default, with the color of `var(--graph-cat-N)` for slot N. Give `cursor` a function that makes your own element, such as an arrow with a name, once for each person; `cursor: false` shows none. They follow the pointer on a flat map and on the globe, and hide behind its horizon. A cursor shows only to people on the same surface, since a point of the canvas and a point of the map are not the same place; selections show on every surface. A row without a `surface`, written by a `create` that leaves it out, shows its cursor everywhere, and its later writes leave the field out too, unless `fields.surface` names one.

```ts
showPresence(graph, {
  collection: crowd,
  me: sessionId,
  create: (id, values) => ({ id, name: userName, ...values }),
  cursor: ({ id }) => cursorFor(crowd.get(id)?.name),
})
```

`presence.store` lists the other people, with their slot and state, and changes only when one joins or leaves: use it for a list of names or of avatars. `presence.stop()` removes the row of the local person, the cursors and the states.

- A row older than `expire`, 30 seconds by default, no longer counts: a person who closed the tab without a trace disappears. The local person rewrites its row every third of that while idle, and removes it when the page closes or the graph is destroyed. Rows are compared with the clock of the client that reads them.
- The local row is written at most every 50 milliseconds, `wait`, and a write that takes long delays the next one by four times as long. Each write sends the whole row of one person, a few dozen bytes, plus the selection cut to `maxSelection` keys, 500 by default.
- The shapes that someone else moves or draws are not drawn apart: with `persistShapes` and `live`, a moved shape already moves on every screen, and a new shape appears when its author ends it.

## Which shapes

`marks` lists the names of the shape marks the editor works on, every shape mark by default. A mark without a `name` cannot be listed: give it one, or leave `marks` out. Shapes of the other marks, and shapes whose geometry cannot be read, take the pointer as without the editor. A graph has at most one editor at a time: `editor.destroy()` gives the pointer and the keys back, and destroying the graph destroys its editor and its layer.

A shape [pinned to a node](/docs/shapes#pin-shapes-to-nodes) moves around its node: its edits keep the geometry relative to the node, and the node does not move.

## On a map

On a [map](/docs/map#shapes), flat or on the globe, the editor takes the gesture from the map: the map does not pan while a shape moves or is drawn, and a double click that ends a shape does not zoom. Shapes in degrees get their edits in degrees, the closing point of a GeoJSON ring included. A circle around a place keeps its radii in meters. On the globe, a circle or a rectangle in world units, such as a shape pinned to a node, faces the screen as the graph draws it, and so do its handles and its text input. A move is a translation on the flat map, on the globe too: a shape moved toward a pole keeps its look on the flat map and grows on the ground, as Web Mercator does.

A graph that [moves onto a map or back](/docs/map#with-and-without-a-map) keeps its editor: a gesture or a drawing in progress is dropped, as with Escape, a text being typed is proposed, and edits waiting for their data stay. With `units: 'auto'`, the next shape is drawn in degrees on the map and in world units on the canvas.

## React

`<ShapeEditor>` and `useShapeEditor` from `@bruno00o/graph/editor/react` create the editor and its layer for the enclosing `<Graph>` or `<MapGraph>`, or for the graph given in `graph`, and destroy them on unmount:

```tsx
import { ShapeEditor } from '@bruno00o/graph/editor/react'

<Graph definition={definition} ariaLabel="Zones">
  <ShapeEditor onEdit={(edits) => setZones((zones) => applyShapeEdits(zones, edits))} />
</Graph>
```

`useShapeEditor` returns the state and the editor, to show the pending edits or the tool, or to undo and remove from buttons. The `tool` prop sets the tool when it changes; `editor.setTool` changes it in between. See [ShapeEditor](/docs/api/shape-editor).

## Keyboard and accessibility

Every move has a key: the arrows move the selected shapes and Delete removes them, so the editor does not need a drag, and every drawn shape takes clicks instead of a drag. The keys reach the editor while the graph element has the focus and a shape is selected, a shape is being drawn, or the key undoes, redoes or duplicates; the other keys go to the graph. A toolbar should give the focus back to the graph element once a tool is chosen, so that Enter and Escape reach the editor. The layer is hidden from assistive technologies. The text input is a `textarea` that takes the focus while it is open, then gives it back to the graph element: set `aria-label` on `view.input`, the view `mountShapeEditor` returns, to give it a name.

## Cost

A graph without an editor runs none of its code: `@bruno00o/graph/editor` is a separate entry point, about 24 kB gzipped and minified, and the graph looks for an editor only on a press or a key. With an editor and nothing selected, a frame costs what it cost before.

Dragging a polygon of 5,000 points costs about 1 millisecond of work per frame in Chrome, 3 at the 95th percentile, to write its preview; the shape follows the pointer one frame later, like a dragged node. Past 5,000 points in the shapes being moved, the editor writes no preview: the graph slides its own shapes on the GPU, with their labels, so that moving 1,000 shapes of 50 points costs about as much per frame as moving the view, and no work on the main thread. Sliding shapes do not answer the pointer until they land. A circle placed in degrees on a map always moves as a preview.

Releasing a moved shape costs 1 to 2 milliseconds: when every point moved by the same amount, the graph keeps the triangles and the coarser copies of the outline, and moves them. Reshaping a polygon of 5,000 points costs about 5 milliseconds more on release, to triangulate it again. Releasing 1,000 moved shapes of 50 points costs about 9 milliseconds.

A change of geometry from your data, such as an edit made by someone else, costs about half a millisecond of work among 10,000 shapes: the graph updates the shapes that changed without sorting the others again, and the next pick finds them where they are. With a selected outline of 1,000 points, its 1,000 handles and 1,000 midpoints add about 0.1 millisecond per frame while the view moves.

Drawing adds nothing measurable to a frame in Chrome: a freehand stroke, and the next segment of a polygon of 500 points, follow the pointer in the next frame, as a hover does. Ending a long freehand stroke costs 1 to 8 milliseconds, to simplify it and write the edit.

Saving costs the write of TanStack DB, which copies and compares the rows it updates, then the reading of the new outlines. In Chrome on an Apple M4 Pro, with a collection in memory: a polygon of 5,000 points takes 2 to 6 milliseconds to write, 100 small shapes moved together 4 to 5, and 1,000 small shapes 26 to 30. A collection in local storage also writes the whole collection to the storage on each write, 7 to 17 milliseconds for the polygon and 63 to 70 for the 1,000 shapes. Another tab receives each write as one frame, 9 to 37 milliseconds after it starts, 80 to 125 for the 1,000 shapes; reading the polygon of 5,000 points there costs 1 to 2 milliseconds, 100 shapes about 1, 1,000 shapes 5 to 10. With `live`, a gesture of a second and a half sends about 16 writes, and 1,000 shapes moved together drop frames: keep `live` for a few shapes at a time. During such a gesture, the graph of the editor reads each write of the shape being reshaped, which it hides behind the preview, but triangulates it only when it shows again: about half a millisecond per write for a polygon of 5,000 points.

Showing others costs the local person one write every 50 milliseconds at most, 15 to 20 a second in Chrome with the pointer moving all the time, and the reading of each row that arrives. With eight other people moving their pointers 20 times a second, 160 row changes a second through a collection in local storage, the main thread of a tab spent about 30 milliseconds more each second, mostly in the collection reading the storage, and frames stayed at 16.7 milliseconds at the 95th percentile. A cursor reached the other tab 12 milliseconds after the write at the median, 11 with the eight others writing, and 15 and 64 milliseconds at the 95th percentile. `@bruno00o/graph/db` grows by about 2.5 kB gzipped, and a graph that does not call `showPresence` runs none of it.

## Not there yet

- Rotating shapes, and snapping to other shapes or to nodes.
- Drawing holes, or several parts in one shape.
- A shape whose geometry comes from a function is edited, but `applyShapeEdits` and `persistShapes` write a field.
- A preview of the shape someone else is drawing, before they finish it.
- Rich text, or a text that follows a line.
