## Declare groups

A group is a node like any other. A node joins it through the `parent` channel, which reads the key of its group. A node whose `parent` is `null` or missing sits at the top level. Groups nest to any depth.

```ts
type Item = { id: string; label: string; parent: string | null }

const items: Item[] = [
  { id: 'g', label: 'Group', parent: null },
  { id: 'h', label: 'Inner group', parent: 'g' },
  { id: 'a', label: 'A', parent: 'g' },
  { id: 'b', label: 'B', parent: 'h' },
]

const definition = defineGraph({
  marks: [
    edges<Link>(links, { source: 'from', target: 'to' }),
    nodes<Item>(items, { parent: 'parent', label: 'label' }),
  ],
})
```

To group items by a field of their data rather than by a `parent` key, use [`groups()`](/docs/transformations#group-items-by-a-field).

A node becomes a group as soon as another node names it. A `parent` that names no node is reported in `issues` as `missing-parent`, and the node waits at the top level until its group arrives. A `parent` that would make a node contain itself is reported as `parent-cycle`, and that node stays at the top level.

## Containers

An unfolded group draws as a container behind its children: a rounded rectangle, or a circle, that fits their discs plus a padding, and follows them as they move. Its label sits above the container. Style it with the `container` option of `nodes()`.

```ts
nodes<Item>(items, {
  parent: 'parent',
  label: 'label',
  container: {
    shape: 'rectangle',
    padding: 24,
    fill: 'var(--graph-group-fill)',
    stroke: 'var(--graph-group-line)',
    strokeWidth: 1,
  },
})
```

The fill and outline default to the `--graph-group-fill` and `--graph-group-line` tokens. States apply to containers too: hovering a container, or selecting it, styles it like a node.

## Spread children

Children of an unfolded group that overlap, such as several nodes at the same coordinates, move apart around their place so that each one can be seen, hovered and dragged. Children at the same place sit on a ring, or on a spiral past six. Children that touch at the current zoom form one pack around their average place, so the packs follow the zoom: they merge as you zoom out and split as you zoom in.

Only the drawn position moves. The data keeps its position: when the group folds, its children go back to their place at once, and `graph.positions.snapshot()` and `persistPositions` never see a spread position. No `positionchange` is emitted.

```ts
groups(items, {
  by: 'site',
  container: { spread: true, gap: 4 },
})
```

- `spread` is on by default. `spread: false` leaves the children where their positions put them.
- `gap` is the space between two spread children: 4 by default, in pixels for children sized with `units: 'screen'`, else in world units. Children in pixels keep their gap at every zoom; children in world units are spread once and never move as you zoom.
- Spreading applies to the direct children of the group that are placed by their data, or by nothing when the definition has no layout. A layout never moves a spread child, and layouts already keep their nodes apart.
- A child you drag, or place with `graph.positions.set`, leaves its pack and stays where you put it; the others close ranks. It joins its pack again when its data moves or when the group folds.
- A hidden child keeps its place in the pack, so the others do not move when it hides or shows.
- Children glide out of their place over `transitions.layout`, or appear at once with reduced motion.
- Past 20,000 spread children sized in pixels, places are computed again at every quarter octave of zoom instead of at every frame: the gaps then vary by about a tenth between two steps.
- Exports draw the positions on screen. An export at another `zoom` keeps the spread of the screen, so gaps in pixels scale with it.

## Fold and unfold

A folded group hides every node inside it and draws as a node, with the options of its mark: color, size, shape, label, badges. Fold groups from code with `graph.groups`, or at mount with `initialFolded`.

```ts
const graph = mountGraph(element, { definition, ariaLabel: 'Groups', initialFolded: ['g'] })

graph.groups.fold(['h'])
graph.groups.unfold(['g'])
graph.groups.toggle('g')
graph.groups.fold()
graph.groups.unfold()
graph.groups.folded()
graph.on('foldchange', ({ folded }) => console.log(folded))
```

`fold()` without keys folds every group, nested ones included, and `unfold()` unfolds them all. A folded group inside a folded group stays folded when its parent opens. A folded key that names no node yet applies when the group arrives.

With `interaction.fold`, on by default, double-clicking a group, or pressing Enter on a focused group, folds or unfolds it. A `doubleclick` handler that calls `originalEvent.preventDefault()` keeps the group as it is.

### Controlled folding

Pass `folded` at mount to own the folded groups: every request, from a double-click, Enter or `graph.groups`, calls `onFoldChange`, and only `update({ folded })` changes what is drawn. As for the [selection](/docs/interaction#controlled-selection), each request starts from the last one not answered yet. In React, pass `folded` and `onFoldChange` to `<Graph>`.

```tsx
const [folded, setFolded] = useState<Key[]>(['g'])

<Graph
  definition={definition}
  ariaLabel="Groups"
  folded={folded}
  onFoldChange={(event) => setFolded([...event.folded])}
/>
```

The folded groups are also in the store, as `folded`, for `useGraphState`. See the [group tree beside the graph](/examples/group-tree) example.

## Merged edges

An edge whose end lies inside a folded group is drawn to the outermost folded group that holds it. The edges of one mark that land on the same two ends, in the same direction, draw as a single aggregated edge. An edge whose two ends land in the same folded group disappears. Unfolding brings every edge back as it was.

The `aggregate` option of `edges()` styles aggregated edges. Its channels and styles read an `AggregateEdge`: the two visible ends, the number of merged edges, their keys and their data.

```ts
edges<Link>(links, {
  source: 'from',
  target: 'to',
  color: 'kind',
  aggregate: {
    width: (aggregate) => aggregate.count,
    color: (aggregate) => aggregate.data[0]?.kind,
    label: (aggregate) => `${aggregate.count}`,
  },
})
```

Without `aggregate`, an aggregated edge takes the look of its first merged edge, and shows a label only when it stands for a single edge. `merge` also merges the parallel edges between visible nodes, both directions on one line if you want: see [Merge parallel edges](/docs/transformations#merge-parallel-edges).

Picking an aggregated edge returns its first edge, with `aggregate` holding all of them. Clicking it selects every merged edge, hovering it hovers them all, and an aggregated edge shows the states of any of its edges.

```ts
graph.on('click', ({ target }) => {
  if (target?.kind === 'edge' && target.aggregate) console.log(target.aggregate.keys)
})
```

## Positions and layouts

- Dragging a container moves every node inside it. Dragging a folded group moves only the group.
- Hidden nodes keep their positions. When a group unfolds, its nodes come back where they were, shifted by how far the group moved while folded, and glide out of the group over `transitions.layout`. Nodes placed by their `x` and `y` channels stay where the data puts them.
- A node that was never shown appears around its group when the group unfolds, then the layout of the definition places it like any new node.
- With a layout in the definition, a group that unfolds makes room for itself: the free nodes and containers it would cover glide out of the way over `transitions.layout`, and so does the group when it is the smaller one. Nodes placed by the data and the node being dragged never move.
- Layouts see a folded group as a single node. Unfolded groups are kept whole at every level of nesting: no container overlaps another container or a node next to it, padding included. See [groups in layouts](/docs/layout#groups) for how each layout does it.
- Fitting the view frames containers whole.
- On a [map](/docs/map#hulls-and-groups), groups follow the map, and the `padding` of a container is in pixels.
- Children that overlap in an unfolded group are [spread](#spread-children) around their place without changing their data.

## Performance

The visible graph is computed from the changes only: folding or unfolding one group costs in proportion to the nodes it hides or shows and to their edges. On 50,000 nodes in 500 groups with 99,000 edges, folding one group takes under a millisecond, and folding all 500 at once about 30 milliseconds plus the redraw of the scene.
