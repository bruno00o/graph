`@bruno00o/graph` draws nodes and edges from your data. You describe the graph once with marks that map fields to visual channels, mount it in an element, and talk to it through a small typed API. The core has no framework dependency; a thin React adapter, two TanStack integrations and a force layout come as separate entry points.

## What it does

- **Declarative marks.** `nodes()` and `edges()` map fields of your data to color, size, labels, arrows and dashes through shared scales. Field names are checked against your data type.
- **Styled labels.** Each label has its own size, color, weight, font and placement, with line breaks and truncation. Edge labels follow their edge and stay upright, and no two labels overlap.
- **Live sources.** Pass an array, or anything with `subscribeChanges`, such as a TanStack DB collection. Only what changed is redrawn.
- **Named states.** Selection, hover and keyboard focus are built in. You declare your own states, style them, and set their members by key.
- **WebGL2 rendering.** 50,000 nodes and 100,000 edges stay smooth, with picking, labels and images. See the [benchmark](/benchmark).
- **Force layout.** Nodes without coordinates settle with a force simulation in a Web Worker, on the GPU for large graphs. New nodes settle next to their neighbors, and dragging a node pins it.
- **Interaction out of the box.** Pan, zoom, drag, single or multiple selection, lasso and keyboard navigation announced to screen readers.
- **Themed with CSS variables.** Light and dark themes ship as a stylesheet; every color in your options can be a CSS variable.

## How it fits together

1. **Describe** the graph with `defineGraph`: marks, scales, states, interaction options. The definition is a frozen, plain value.
2. **Mount** it with `mountGraph(container, { definition, ariaLabel })`, or render `<Graph>` in React.
3. **Respond** to events such as `click` or `selectionchange`, read the store, and drive the view, the selection and your states through the instance.
